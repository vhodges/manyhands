## Purpose And Authority

This is the fourth Cycle of Wave 06. In a pull-request repository, `document promote` synchronizes and publishes the document's branch and answers with a link to open its pull request, never committing to or pushing the primary; the only commits on the branch are the synchronization's own merges. Promoting again while the link is outstanding synchronizes again and answers `already_applied` with the same link. A document counts as promoted only when the content proof shows that a primary holds the branch's content, so a squash counts and an edit of a merged document does not count until its own pull request merges; `document show` and `item sync` offer the link until then.

## Departures From The RFC

- The RFC's Approach A gives the pull-request path "its own runner that close and promote hand off to": the pull-request promote is a mode of the existing promotion lifecycle instead (decision 1), as Cycle 03's decision 13 made the close.
- The RFC's "Promoting again while the link is outstanding answers `already_applied`, with the same link": it synchronizes first, so saves made since reach the pull request (decision 2).
- The RFC's "`item sync` is unchanged": it gives the link for a document with an outstanding link (decision 4), as Cycle 03's decision 12 does for a closing ticket.
- The RFC's "An outstanding link ends when its item is reported done, or when its branch is retired or abandoned": it ends when the content proof shows the primary holds the document (decision 3), or by retirement. No verb abandons a published document context (K-4).
- The RFC's "The clone that gave a link keeps it in its registry, so that `repo inspect`, `ticket show`, `document show` and the desktop can offer it again": `repo inspect` and the desktop offer it from Cycle 06 (decision 12, K-10).
- The CLI contract's known limit of decision 35 and Cycle 03's close keep `history_unknown` as a prepare blocker: the pull-request promote does not (decision 10).
- The RFC does not say what promote answers once the primary holds the document: `already_applied` with no link, at prepare when this clone has seen the merge (design R-4).

## Required Contract

1. **(owner's eye)** In a pull-request repository, `document promote` synchronizes the document's branch with its remote copy and the remote's primary and publishes it, as `item sync` does, and answers with the `pull_request` link. It writes nothing on the branch beyond that synchronization, and no command changed by this Cycle commits to or pushes the local or the remote primary in a pull-request repository (except a direct promote accepted before the repository switched, K-8). In direct mode `document promote` is unchanged.
2. The pull-request promote's prepare, digest and steps are the design's Parts B and C, and its behaviour is the outcomes C1 to C29, the two answer tables, and the required outcomes of a re-run (RR1 to RR8) and of the retirement fence (F1 to F4). The consent digest binds the mode, so a prepare made in one mode cannot be confirmed in the other.
3. **(owner's eye)** Promoting again while the link is outstanding synchronizes again, publishing any saves since and merging the remote's primary in, and answers `already_applied` with the same link, the one first given; its effects show what it published (decision 2).
4. **(owner's eye)** In a pull-request repository a document counts as promoted only when the primary holds it by the content proof (design R-3), never by presence or ancestry alone. Once it does, `document promote` answers `already_applied` with no link: at prepare when this clone has already fetched the merge, with no data and nothing written; at confirm with `primary_holds` true, after only the synchronization's own merge and push. A confirm whose prepare was made before this clone fetched the merge answers `external_change`, and a new prepare then answers `already_applied` (design R-29).
5. **(owner's eye)** `document show` and `document show --path` of a document read from its active context report the link as `pull_request` while it is outstanding, and null once the primary holds the document (decisions 3 and 9); lists keep it null. Either retirement path deletes the link row.
6. `item sync` of a document context in a pull-request repository that publishes or finds current a branch with an outstanding link answers with the link (decision 4). It never writes the link row.
7. **(owner's eye)** The pull-request promote is a mode of the existing document-promotion lifecycle (decision 1), with its rules: a step that stops on its synchronization answers as `item sync` answers that stop and leaves the promote unfinished; the same request continues it; `request cancel` and Ctrl-C answer `cancelled` and leave it unfinished; a new request's prepare is held by it; a finished one replays its answer; `operation list` lists it as a `promote`. While it is unfinished, every retirement path skips or refuses its document (decision 13, F1 to F4). A re-run continues over its child's own moves; before any attempt of its child pushed, a commit its child did not make, or a changed remote, endpoint or forge setting, ends it as a refused record (`external_change`) with nothing published; once its child has completed, it finishes without the network or the live configuration; a child held by a conflict or an identity window continues to its own end by its way out (decisions 14 and 15, RR1 to RR8).
8. **(owner's eye)** A remote branch deleted without a merge answers `remote_branch_deleted`, whose way out is `item republish`, then a new promote (decision 11). `history_unknown` does not block the pull-request prepare (decision 10).
9. The answer's data is the new `pull_request_promotion` shape and the preview's the new `pull_request_promotion_preview`; there is no new result code, blocker, verb or operation action.
10. Direct mode is otherwise unchanged. Every existing answer, golden and test keeps its result, except the named edits, the record's new columns, and `document show`'s `pull_request` for a document that has a link row.

## Product-Owner Decisions (2026-11-26)

1. **A mode of the promotion lifecycle.** The pull-request promote is a `document_promotions` row in `pull_request` mode, run by Cycle 03's branch path with the context child only: no integration, no publication child, no cleanup. Attempts, cancellation, replay, attribution and listing are the lifecycle's. Chosen over a hand-off to `item sync`'s binding, the separate-runner shape Cycle 03 left (its decision 13).
2. **Promoting again synchronizes, then answers `already_applied`.** Confirm runs the synchronization again, publishing any saves since and merging the remote's primary in, and answers `already_applied` with the same link; the effects show what was published. Chosen over a prepare that answers with the link and synchronizes nothing.
3. **The link ends by the content proof.** `document show` and promote treat the link as outstanding only while the content proof fails against the local and the tracking primary; once it passes, `show` gives `pull_request` null and promote answers `already_applied` with no link. Retirement still deletes the row, as Cycle 03 built. Chosen over a link that ends only when its row is deleted.
4. **`item sync` gives a document's link.** When `item sync` publishes, or finds current, a document branch with an outstanding link in a pull-request repository, it answers with Cycle 03's `pull_request_link` data. Only promote gives the first link.
5. **Cycle 03 is a dependency.** vh-gy8gz becomes a dependency of vh-rphcs, because this Cycle builds on its link record, item field, branch path, withdraw prepare and `item sync` link; Task 0 starts after it merges.
6. **No new SSH case.** Cycle 03's one pull-request SSH case gains promote and promote-again steps for a document, which write the success golden and show the server's primary unchanged.
7. **The design outline** (Parts A to G) was approved for drafting.
8. **Managed documents.** These documents are authored as Manyhands managed documents and published with `item sync`, so the owner reviews them in the desktop; the execution ledger stays on the ticket branch (the owner's instruction from Cycle 02's planning, 2026-11-25).
9. **A cheaper check in `document show`** (after review round 1). Show applies the content proof's rule to the trees' entries without loading blobs, and finds the document by its path; promote and `item sync` keep the full proof. Chosen over the full proof everywhere, which reads every blob of two trees and parses every Markdown item on each show.
10. **`history_unknown` does not block the pull-request prepare** (after review round 1). A first push leaves no tracking ref, so the usual path, `item sync` and then promote, would otherwise need a poll first; the confirm's fetch settles the history, and a deleted branch answers `remote_branch_deleted` at confirm. This departs from the CLI contract's known limit (its decision 35) and from Cycle 03's close.
11. **`remote_branch_deleted`'s way out is `item republish`** (after review round 1), then a new promote, keyed on the new data so that the direct promote, the close and `item sync` keep theirs. The skill says that if the pull request did merge, cleanup is Cycle 05's.
12. **`repo inspect`'s listing of outstanding links goes to Cycle 06** (after review round 1), for tickets and documents, with the desktop's link actions.
13. **Retirement is fenced while a pull-request promotion is unfinished** (after review round 2; stated as the design's table F from revision 4). The poll's, `context retire`'s and the desktop's retirement skip a document whose pull-request promotion record is unfinished, as they already skip a ticket whose closure is unfinished; re-running the request then finds the branch deleted and the primary holding the document, answers `already_applied`, and the next poll retires it. Chosen over recording the consented tip and ending through `ContextGone`.
14. **A stopped promote that changed since consent ends as `external_change`** (after review round 2; refined from revision 4 by the design's table RR, whose window is "before any attempt of its child pushed" and whose checks are a commit its child did not make and a changed remote name or endpoint, and by decision 15). Before its child completes, a re-run whose publication remote is no longer the configured one, or whose branch tip is no longer the consented one, ends as a refused record (`external_change`), with nothing published by it; a new prepare starts over. Once the child has published, the promote finishes regardless, because the rest needs no network. Chosen over leaving it to Cycle 03's runner rule, under which it would wait for good.
15. **A held child continues to its own end** (after review round 4). While a conflict or an identity window holds the promote's child, which neither `request cancel` nor the runner can let go, the child continues by its way out (resolving the conflict publishes the resolution, as any `item sync` conflict's does), and the promote then finishes; the changed-since-consent checks apply only to a child that can be let go. A known limit (K-16). Chosen over making the conflict continuation check for changes before it pushes, which would touch the conflict verbs and the remote reservation and add the six remote SSH targets.
16. **Approval** (2026-11-26). The owner approved the three documents at revision 7 without a further review round, including decisions 13 and 14 as refined by the design's tables RR and F and decision 15; approved the named test edits E-1 to E-14; chose subagent-driven execution, with Sonnet 5.5 at high effort as the implementer; and authorized implementation. Cycle 03 has merged (integrated at `6bc4b69c`), so Task 0 starts on this approval.
17. **A held child and a changed remote** (2026-11-27, at Task 4's design checkpoint). While a conflict holds a stopped promote's child and the configured publication remote differs from the child's, the conflict verbs refuse, as for any synchronization's conflict, and the promote keeps waiting; once the remote is configured back, the conflict resolves and the promote finishes (RR6, then RR2). C29's rename row renames back before resolving. Chosen over letting the conflict verbs resolve against the conflicted operation's own remote, which would change `remote/sync.rs` and the remote reservation (decision 15).
18. **A branch removed by hand while a conflict holds the promote** (2026-11-27). RR7 cannot end a record whose child is retained, since a retained child cannot be let go and the record cannot be abandoned over it: the promote answers `recovery_required` and waits, and restoring the branch and its worktree lets the conflict's way out continue. A known limit, K-19. Chosen over releasing the held child, which would change the reservation's release rule.
19. **A change registered while a conflict is pending** (2026-11-27, refining 17). Any poll or synchronization registers a changed remote name, primary branch or SSH URL, and from then on neither a child's restart nor the conflict verbs take up the older child, even after the change is undone. Read from the source, this is so today for every conflicted synchronization (a plain `item sync`, a direct promote's child, a Cycle 03 close's child), which then blocks every synchronization in the repository for good. This Cycle changes nothing in `remote/` for it: K-16 says the change must be undone before anything registers it, the C29 rows run "change, change back, resolve" with no call in between, and the pre-existing defect is the ticket vh-zn1fd (`01M8EAKJPNER2P0G9MS8V6187N`). Chosen over fixing it inside this Cycle, which would touch the remote reservation and the conflict verbs and add the six remote SSH targets.

## Known Limits

- K-1: The link is kept per clone (RFC). Another clone that promotes the same document gives and records its own.
- K-2: After a forge merge whose remote branch survives, cleanup waits for Cycle 05's confirmed deletion; meanwhile promote answers `already_applied` (at prepare with no data, at confirm with `primary_holds`), and `document show` gives no link.
- K-3: A squash after which the primary changed the same document again gives the context child a merge conflict; the person resolves it, or waits for Cycle 05's confirmed manual cleanup.
- K-4: No verb abandons a published document context (`context release` refuses one), so the link ends by the content proof or by retirement only; Cycle 05's manual cleanup adds a third ending.
- K-5: A promote whose child stopped holds, until its request runs again, what an unfinished promotion holds today: the document's prepare, a change of the repository's setting, and `context sync-local` of every item; and, by decision 13, the document's retirement. Neither it nor its child can be abandoned; the same request finishes it.
- K-6: The proof is as fresh as the last fetch: until a poll or a synchronization fetches the merge, `document show` still gives the link.
- K-7: Until Cycle 06 the desktop's "Approve and merge" in a pull-request repository shows a generic preview, and the answer's link is not offered as actions.
- K-8: A direct promote accepted before the repository switched to pull-request mode finishes in direct mode, and pushes the primary, as Cycle 01 decision 11 says for every operation.
- K-9: After a forge merge whose remote branch survives, a new edit saved on that branch makes the next promote an `again`: it answers `already_applied` with the first link, whose page opens a new pull request from the same branch; the skill says so. Cycle 05's cleanup of the surviving branch removes the case.
- K-10: Until Cycle 06, `repo inspect` does not list outstanding links (decision 12); `document show` and `ticket show` give them.
- K-11: `document show`'s cheaper check can disagree with the full proof in a repository with missing objects, a document ID held at two paths, a primary copy that does not parse, or a document moved on the primary (decision 9).
- K-12: After a squash on which the primary moved on, a deleted branch answers `remote_branch_deleted`, and its way out, `item republish`, would revive a branch whose pull request merged; the skill points to Cycle 05's confirmed manual cleanup instead.
- K-13: After the publication remote or the forge setting changes between two promotes, `again` gives the first link, built for the old setting, until the row ends (design R-17).
- K-14: After a stopped promote finishes as `already_applied`, the poll retires the document only when the root is on a clean primary, as every retirement needs today (Cycle 05 adds the warning).
- K-15: Until a poll prunes it, prepare and `document show` read a stale tracking ref of a branch the forge deleted, so a document whose remote copy was ahead reads not held there; confirm reads it as absent (design R-3).
- K-16: A change of the publication remote, or a save, while a conflict or an identity window holds a stopped promote's child is not caught: the child continues to its own end by its way out, and resolving the conflict publishes the resolution as any conflict's is published (decision 15). While the configured remote differs from the child's, the conflict verbs refuse as for any synchronization's conflict and the promote waits; once the remote is configured back, the conflict resolves and the promote finishes (decision 17). A change that a poll or a synchronization registers before it is undone strands the child for good, as it strands any synchronization's conflict today (decision 19, vh-zn1fd).
- K-17: The link of a first promote is built when its record finishes, from the remote URL and forge setting as they are then, so a change of either after the confirm can give a link other than the previewed one (design R-30).
- K-18: Once a pull-request promote's branch is gone, a same-request replay of its finished answer answers `recovery_required` unless the primary holds the commit the answer names: a merge commit or a fast-forward replays, a squash does not, as Cycle 03's K-13 says for a close.
- K-19: If the document's branch and its worktree are removed by hand while a conflict holds a stopped promote's child, the promote answers `recovery_required` and waits; restoring the branch and its worktree lets the conflict's way out continue (decision 18). The same answer comes while the acknowledgement of an unowned child's cancellation fails, until it succeeds.
- K-20: A rename of the publication remote while an attempt of a stopped promote's child may have pushed leaves the promote waiting until the remote is configured back, as decision 17 rules for a held child. And a process lost between the promote's last head check and its child's own push check, with a save landing in that instant, reads as an attempt that may have pushed, so the next call publishes the save (decision 14's one residual; closing it needs a change to `remote/sync.rs`).
